# Screen draft v2: brief (approved by Alex, Nov 6)

## Why v2

Alex's feedback on draft v1 (`design/draft/`): it looks like an animation explaining the event, not a real system an astronaut would use.

- A real system would not put the map in the center with arrows showing where the dots move.
- A real system would not just say "Everyone within their normal range"; it shows real status.
- Crew cannot be just names: they need photos.

## Direction: a real operations system with an emergency mode

Model it on Airbus cockpit displays: when something fails, the system automatically brings up the right page and the checklist. For the medical view, model it on hospital patient monitors. Decision 13 ("things appear as needed") becomes this mode switch, not an animation.

### Normal mode (03:31, before the alert)

Real operations software, dense but orderly:

- **Status bar:** mission day and ship time, Earth link state with one-way delay 12:40, alarm count, which display this is (for example "Lab display").
- **Crew list with portraits:** name, role, location, live mini vitals (heart rate, SpO2, temperature), sleep state, sensor status and last sync time.
- **Habitat systems strip:** cabin O2, CO2, pressure, temperature, power, water.
- **Habitat plan** as one panel among others, not the hero: crew positions, supply markers, medical inventory summary.
- **Event log** with timestamps and routine entries. Include "03:20 Galley: food pack opened (Alex)": it quietly plants the trigger that the report names later.

### Emergency mode (from step a1)

The screen switches and everything unrelated dims:

- **Alarm banner:** "MEDICAL ALERT · ALEX · LAB", with time and level. No diagnosis before Argo has asked (design principle 4).
- **Alex's patient monitor, large:** ECG and pleth waveforms, numerics with alarm limits, trends against his personal baseline band, SpO2 projection to 90%.
- **Clocks:** Earth sees this in 12:40 and earliest reply 25:20 from a1; the red "Oxygen below 90%" clock from a4.
- **Procedure page** (from a3): matched signals, steps, current step highlighted, "do not delay epinephrine" note.
- **Crew response** (from a5): Maria (crew medical officer) alerted with a timestamp, her route and ETA; Stelios and Giannis not woken (two hatches away).
- **Habitat plan in guidance view** (from a5): the route as real wayfinding with hatch IDs and distance (for example "Lab to Node 2 via hatch L-N2 · Locker N3"), Lab kit flagged empty (used day 112), Locker N3 with 2 injectors, then 1 after a7. No story arrows.
- **Event log** keeps running with a timestamped entry for each step.
- **Report** (a8): auto-generated report card, transmit status, arrives at Earth in 12:40.

### Argo's voice

A transcript panel with timestamps, like a radio log. The current Argo line is large enough to read from the back of the room; earlier lines are smaller. Alex's replies show as "voice response received" with a waveform, not as text, because the actor's live words may differ from the script.

### Source highlighting

When Argo speaks, the items behind the line highlight in place (a linked highlight), as a real assistant UI would.

## Review of draft v2 (Alex, Nov 7)

- Keep: a good template. It combines simplicity while still looking like a system.
- Fix: it does not feel alive. The heart lines do not look like they move, and the curves are small. Cause: the waves are drawn like a bedside monitor sweep, where only a narrow gap moves across the strip, and every beat is identical, so the redrawn trace looks unchanged; the amplitude is also low.
- Make it alive: visibly moving waveforms (a bright write point, or scrolling) with beat-to-beat variation and larger amplitude; values that update every second or two with small realistic changes; trends and crew sparklines that grow in real time; sync indicators that blink on each update; new log entries that slide in.
- Make events unmistakable: the judges must see at once when something happens. Key moments get a large on-screen announcement (Alex's examples: WARNING, CONTACTING EARTH), which then settles into the banner and panels. The event log keeps the detail; it is never the only place an event shows.
- Direction: build the real app, with a trigger page that fires events and a main screen that responds live (decided Nov 7, docs/05 decision 4; open decisions B and C).

## Live version (Nov 7, after Alex's review)

Built in `design/draft-v2/` (the same files plus `announce.js` and `portraits/`). Layout and the Day 143 numbers are unchanged; everything now runs in real time.

- **Waves:** a bedside-monitor sweep with a bright write point, a short erase gap and the newest trace brightest. Every beat differs a little (breathing-linked rhythm, R and T wave heights, baseline drift), the curves fill most of the strip, and the strips are slightly taller. A heart symbol flashes on every beat and a pulse bar rises with every pulse.
- **Numbers:** heart rate every second, SpO2 every 2, breathing every 4, temperature every 10. They follow one continuous simulated curve keyed to ship time (`vitals.js`), so trends, numbers and waves always agree. Crew tiles update at each sync (1 to 3 s) with a blinking sync light; the systems strip drifts and blinks too.
- **Trends and sparklines grow:** the time axis is anchored when a state starts and the line grows toward the right edge, with a ping on each new point. The oxygen projection shortens as time passes and its label counts down.
- **Log and transcript:** entries slide in at their ship time. Argo's line appears whole (readable from the back) with a sweep over the words as they are spoken, then "Listening" and the crew's voice entry.
- **Announcements:** MEDICAL ALERT (amber) and CONTACTING EARTH (Argo violet) at the alarm, WARNING (red) at the oxygen projection, REPORT TO EARTH at the report. Each is a band across the middle that flashes like an annunciator, holds about 2 seconds, then shrinks into the banner, clocks or report panel that keeps the detail. The event log records each one. - **Map:** route lines flow toward the locker; Maria walks her route with a live ETA (03:33:40 to 03:34:05); Alex's dot walks to Locker N3 during his answer (03:33:49 to 03:33:55).
- **Ship clock:** the alarm is raised at 03:32:44, so the two announcements finish before Argo's first line at 03:32:50. All other times are unchanged (dose 03:34:02, report 03:34:13).
- **Controls:** keys 1 to 4 play each state from its key moment; Right arrow, Space or Page Down step forward (a presentation clicker sends Page Down); `#play` loops the whole scenario (about 70 s); `?still` gives the frozen states for screenshots, and `&ann=medical|earth|warning|report` a frozen announcement (`argo-v2-5-warning.png`). *Replaced Nov 7 by the keyboard run below.*

## Sound (Nov 7, in scope per docs/05)

Built in `design/draft-v2/sound.js` and wired into `app.js` and `announce.js`. Layout, timings and the Day 143 numbers are unchanged. The only visual additions: an AUDIO PAUSED mark in the alarm banner, a "Sound off" mark in the status bar while sound is off, and a short note on screen when a sound key is pressed.

- **All synthesized** in the browser with the Web Audio API: no audio files, nothing copied from films, games or real devices, so it works offline and raises no licensing questions. If a file is ever needed: CC0 only, credited in the README.
- **Pulse tone:** a quiet beep on every R wave of the ECG strip (from the same beat generator, so sound and trace agree). Its pitch follows the SpO2 on the monitor, one semitone per point (880 Hz at 100%, 659 Hz at 95, 622 Hz at 94), so the room hears the oxygen fall and then recover. Real oximeters work the same way with a smaller step that each maker chooses; ours is larger so a room can hear it.
- **Alarms** follow IEC 60601-1-8: MEDICAL ALERT plays a medium-priority burst (3 pulses), WARNING a high-priority burst (10 pulses: 3, 2, pause, 3, 2), about 5 dB louder. Each starts with its announcement and sounds once. When the band lands, the banner shows AUDIO PAUSED with a crossed bell, as on a monitor whose alarm audio is paused. A new, higher alarm (the WARNING) sounds again. Only one burst each, because a second would land on CONTACTING EARTH (alert) or on Argo's first words (warning).
  - Candidate A: one pitch per priority, like a classic bedside monitor.
  - Candidate B: the standard's melodies, cardiac (C E G, rising) for the alert and oxygen (C B A, G F, falling) for the warning.
- **Earth link:** NASA's Apollo Quindar tones (2525 Hz as the link opens, 2475 Hz as it closes, 250 ms each). CONTACTING EARTH: opening tone, a short data burst, closing tone. REPORT TO EARTH: opening tone and a longer burst as sending starts. When the bar reaches "Sent", the closing tone and a soft two-note confirmation play, held until Argo finishes speaking.
  - Candidate A: radio static and a packet-radio data burst (Bell 202 tones, the sound of APRS packets).
  - Candidate B: clean digital pings with a soft echo.
- **Argo:** a subtle two-note chirp as it starts speaking, a soft upward glide as it starts listening.
- **Room for the voice and the actor:** every effect drops to 30% while Argo speaks; while Argo listens, only the pulse tone plays. Argo's voice (see "Voice") plays in the mix next to the chirps, after that duck.
- **Cabin hum (optional):** very low ventilation noise, off by default.
- **Controls:** Chrome plays sound only after a key press or click, so the first one turns sound on ("Sound off" shows until then). M turns sound off and on. With `?try` in the address, A switches the alarm candidate and E the Earth candidate on the calm screen, each playing a sample (the alert then the warning; the contact then the confirmation); without it, A and E do nothing. H toggles the cabin hum. One config at the top of `sound.js`: master volume, a volume per category (monitor, alarm, earth, argo, cabin), the duck level, the pulse pitch step and the chosen candidates.
- **Picked by ear (Alex, Nov 7; docs/05 decision 19):** alarm A and Earth link A, the defaults in `sound.js`. A and E now need `?try`, so a stray press on the calm screen during the opening talk cannot play an alarm sample. Test on the venue speakers on Day 2 morning (docs/05).

- **The heart-rate rule (MED-07).** After epinephrine, once oxygen and breathing are back inside their limits, a heart rate up to 120 counts as the drug's expected effect, not as an alarm. Until then a fast heart could still be the reaction, so it stays an alarm. Epinephrine's blood level peaks 5 to 10 minutes after a thigh injection and it raises heart rate; care teams widen the heart-rate limit after giving it, and 120 is a common adult monitor limit. Heart rate itself is unchanged from item 7: it stays up and eases slowly. Sources in docs/06 ("After the dose").
- **Advisory, not all clear.** The event stays open, as in a clinic: under observation, reassessment at 5 minutes, second injector ready (guidelines observe for hours after anaphylaxis). Colors follow IEC 60601-1-8: red high, amber medium, cyan low priority.
- **Pace.** Real recovery takes a few minutes; the stage compresses it to about a minute, as the script already does when Alex breathes easier seconds after the dose. Breathing is a slow average, so after the dose it settles without jitter: once it reads 20 it never reads 21 again.
- **Changes inside the sketch.** The breathing number shows amber with ABOVE LIMIT from the alarm on (it is 22 to 24 throughout), during line 8 SpO2 reads 94 or 95 RECOVERING instead of 96, and the report card's last SpO2 is a point lower.
