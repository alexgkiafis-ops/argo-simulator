# Argo: NASA Space Apps 2026 (health monitoring challenge)

We are building **Argo**, an onboard assistant for a Mars transit crew. It combines an astronaut's vitals with knowledge of the ship (map, crew positions, medical supplies) and talks the crew through an emergency in the window before Earth can answer. **Since Nov 7, night (decision 30), the focus is the simulator hosted on Vercel:** anyone opens the link, sees the live monitoring screen, picks a scenario that plays by itself ("Day 143", a severe allergic reaction, or since Nov 8 "Day 128", a CO2 pocket in a sleep station, decision 38) and watches how Argo handles it. The team presents it as argo-simulator.com (the main address since Nov 8, decision 33; argos-simulator.com also works), an open-source project that simulates real space scenarios and how Argo would act in them. Before that, the deliverable was a 7-minute pitch whose centerpiece is a live sketch of the same app, with Alex answering it on stage and the steps fired unseen by keybinds (decisions 4, 15 and 29); that flow lives on as the presentation mode (decision 31).

Alex knows he tends to scope-creep. Act as the guardian:

## Ground rules

- **HELIOS is reference only.** Read it, never build on it. Never copy its assets (fonts, 3D models, photos, videos). If any code is copied, keep its MIT notice in that file. Credit it in the README and submission references (line in docs/04).
- **Open source.** The repository and the site will be public: never put the ElevenLabs key or anything secret in them (`make-site.js` stops if something that looks like a key is in the site folder), and keep `reference/` (HELIOS, another team's code, git-ignored) out of anything published.
- **Honesty.** Label simulated data as simulated. Never present scripted behavior as live AI. The pitch calls the app a prototype.
- **Consistency.** Every number Argo says must match the screen at that moment (see `consistency_constraints` in the timeline JSON).

- `./design/draft-v2`: the app (decision 21): `argo-screen-v2.html` (it also opens from a file in Chrome, offline), with `app.js`, `day143.js`, `day128.js` and `world-day128.js` (the second scenario, decision 38: its script and what the monitor shows for it; its voices in `voice/en/day128-en.js`, loaded when a visitor picks it), `scenarios.js` (the scenarios in the strip and the operator's word), `vitals.js`, `sound.js`, `announce.js`, `style.css`, `fonts/`, `portraits/` and `voice/en/` (Argo's voice, `voice-en.js`, Giannis's take of line 1, `voice-en-giannis.js`, loaded when the operator types G I A N N I S, and the patient's for scene 2, `nikos-en.js`), and `slides.js` with `slides/` (the pitch slides on S, decision 36). Online, `tools/site/make-site.js` publishes `argo-screen-v2.html` as `index.html`. No `./app` folder and no build step.
- `./tools/voice`: `make-voice.js` makes Argo's voice files and the patient's answers for scene 2 from `day143.js` (with `--scenario day128`, Day 128's from `day128.js`, into `voice/en/day128-en.js`); `cache/` keeps each file's text and timestamps so unchanged lines are reused
- `./design/voice-auditions`: the 24 audition clips for Argo (12 voices, lines 1 and 4), in `nikos/` 18 for the patient's voice (6 voices, answers 1, 3 and 7; the patient was Nikos then), and in `giannis/` the seven spellings of Argo's line 1 to Giannis (take 1, "Giannis" as written, is the one in the app; decision 34)
- `./tools/site`: `make-site.js` builds `../argo-online/`, the folder deployed to Vercel (project argos-simulator, live at https://argo-simulator.com, the main address, decision 33, and at https://argos-simulator.vercel.app; the command is in the file); it stops if anything that looks like an API key is in it
- `./tools/slides`: `export-slides.ps1` exports the pitch deck (default: `new_local_slides.pptx` in Downloads) through PowerPoint into `design/draft-v2/slides/` (decision 36)
- `./design/portraits-first-cast`: the first cast's illustrated portraits, no longer used
- `./reference/helios`: cloned reference, git-ignored
- `./docs`, `./spec`: this plan
